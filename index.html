Bucher & Rogers Family Tree

Family history research for the Bucher and Rogers families

The Bucher and Rogers families of Franklin and Bedford Counties, Tennessee. All eight great-grandparents of Danny and Brenda Bucher's children are named; the Bucher side reaches 1500s Netherlands, 1600s England and Virginia, and 1700s canton Bern in Switzerland, with a documented Revolutionary War colonel, and the Rogers side now reaches colonial North Carolina, including a Revolutionary War pensioner. Col. Daniel Gillespie is the most likely patriot behind Grandma Ludye's DAR membership, since his line down to her is documented. Any family member who has her DAR papers could confirm it, and they would make a strong starting point for a new DAR or SAR application on this line.

On the Rogers side, Michael Awalt is the strongest new candidate: his own 1832 pension declaration, made in Franklin County, proves his service. An application would need records tying each generation from Mary Polly Awalt (b. 1830) back to him. No DAR ancestor number for him turned up online; the DAR Genealogical Research System at dar.org is the place to check.

More on Michael Awalt, from his pension file and North Carolina records (documented unless noted). He was born in Pennsylvania and didn't know the year. He served his first tours (Ninety Six, 1775, and Cross Creek, 1776) while an apprentice in Rowan Co., NC, sent by the man he was bound to, who isn't named, and he became free at 21 (pension W326). The 1790 Mecklenburg Co. census lists him as Michael Evalt in the German district that became Cabarrus County, with an older Jacob Evalt household next door and a Widow Speck in the same district. He and his wife Eva appear in the register of St. John's Lutheran Church, Buffalo Creek, Cabarrus Co.: their daughter Elizabeth was born Oct 8, 1799 and baptized Nov 24, 1799 (FamilySearch ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-C33K-L6B6-R), and in 1797 he sponsored a baptism there. On Feb 4, 1800 Deobald Speck deeded him about 70 acres on Buffalo Creek, part of David Speck's 1762 grant, and the same day he sold John Misenheimer his own tract plus 20 acres of the Speck grant, probably before moving west (Cabarrus deeds on FamilySearch). In 1851 his son Jacob Awalt, about 62, of Franklin Co., TN, declared that his mother Eva had married Michael about 1786 and died Aug 2, 1848, and named her heirs: Catherine Weber, Jacob Awalt, John Awalt, Barby Pins (read as Barbry Tipps in the pension transcription), Sophia Webb and Nancy Limbo (FamilySearch ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-99XP-Q94V-K). Elizabeth, baptized in 1799, isn't in that list; she may have died young. None of this changes the Medium rating: the step from Jacob C. Awalt to Polly still needs a record.

Josiah Brandon's father, Capt. Thomas Brandon, fought for the Crown and was mortally wounded at Kings Mountain (Josiah's pension; the name Thomas comes from William Doub Bennett's 1981 study of Burke Co. land records). He was a Loyalist, so he is not a patriot ancestor for DAR or SAR purposes.

Civil War relatives

For family members interested in joining the United Daughters of the Confederacy (UDC) or Sons of Confederate Veterans (SCV), as of Oct 29, 2026; relations are counted from Danny and Brenda's children. George Fletcher Rogers is now the best direct-line candidate, and George S. Weddington the best uncle-line one

The UDC accepts direct descendants of a Confederate soldier, or of his brother. His service has to be honorable, with no desertion, and he can't have taken the Union oath of allegiance before April 9, 1865. A tombstone isn't accepted as proof. A National Archives service record, a state pension file, or an official published roster is.

No service record turned up for the Bucher, Mason, Coots, Withers, Parks, Silvertooth, Jackson, Pope, Puckett, Crawford, Gillespie or Harris men, for Lewis Anderson (b. 1814), or for Simon Peter Bugher (b. 1832), who lived in Ohio. Pleasant Neville (b. 1806) and Samuel Kaesermann (b. 1807) were too old to be likely soldiers. Simon Bucher's uncle John Kaserman served as a Union captain from Ohio, per WikiTree, so he doesn't count for the UDC. Men who died before 1891 wouldn't appear in Tennessee's pension index, though, so that doesn't mean none of them served. A James M. Rogers grave at Estill Springs is also listed as Confederate; FamilySearch has a James M. Rogers (1826–1863) as another brother of George Fletcher Rogers, but that match is unproven.

Why George Fletcher Rogers is probably the 45th Tennessee soldier: the index places the pensioner in Franklin County, the widow's first name and initial (Pamelia F.) match his second wife Permelia Frances, she lived in his household in 1900 and outlived him, and his grave carries a Confederate listing. The index lists out-of-state units by state (for example "22nd Miss. Inf."), so a plain "45th Inf." means a Tennessee regiment. What's still missing: no marriage record for George and Permelia has been found yet, and only the pension file itself would show his company, and whether he was paroled, took the oath or deserted. That matters for the UDC.
