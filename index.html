Bucher & Rogers Family Tree

Family history research for the Bucher and Rogers families

The Bucher and Rogers families of Franklin and Bedford Counties, Tennessee. All eight great-grandparents of Danny and Brenda Bucher's children are named; the Bucher side reaches 1500s Netherlands, 1600s England and Virginia, and 1700s canton Bern in Switzerland, with a documented Revolutionary War colonel, and the Rogers side now reaches colonial North Carolina, including a Revolutionary War pensioner. Built from 23andMe, the family's MyHeritage tree (Hosler Family Site), a 2018 Ancestry.com family tree, the FamilySearch Family Tree, WikiTree, family Bibles, wills, obituaries and grave records, with each claim checked.

Confidence ratings and source links are kept for each claim. Research status as of Oct 28, 2026.

Col. Daniel Gillespie is the most likely patriot behind Grandma Ludye's DAR membership, since his line down to her is documented. Any family member who has her DAR papers could confirm it, and they would make a strong starting point for a new DAR or SAR application on this line.

Civil War relatives

For family members interested in joining the United Daughters of the Confederacy (UDC) or Sons of Confederate Veterans (SCV), as of Oct 29, 2026; relations are counted from Danny and Brenda's children

The UDC accepts direct descendants of a Confederate soldier, or of his brother. His service has to be honorable, with no desertion, and he can't have taken the Union oath of allegiance before April 9, 1865. A tombstone isn't accepted as proof. A National Archives service record, a state pension file, or an official published roster is.

No service record turned up for the Bucher, Mason, Coots, Withers, Parks, Silvertooth, Jackson, Pope, Puckett, Crawford, Gillespie or Harris men, for Lewis Anderson (b. 1814), or for Simon Peter Bugher (b. 1832), who lived in Ohio. Simon Bucher's uncle John Kaserman served as a Union captain from Ohio, per WikiTree, so he doesn't count for the UDC. Men who died before 1891 wouldn't appear in Tennessee's pension index, though, so that doesn't mean none of them served.
